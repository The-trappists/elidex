Method, terms and limits
Dataset
— threads, — network elements, — ground-truth facts in — knowledge threads (— of all threads). Generated by ctx dataset generate --scale large, seed 42. Everything is invented.
Why scale matters
A TX-3000 node is covered by a median of — valid facts on 1 June 2026. Retrieval by element and time alone returns all of them, whatever the problem.
Knowledge base
Every knowledge thread, in time order, through the real linker, store and lifecycle, with the ground truth as the extractor. This isolates linking, time and retrieval from the LLM, which the labelled set measures.
Cases
Built per fact: a similar case on the class, a near miss on the same model, another unit at the same site, after the knowledge was closed, before it was said, a dispatch, plus random elements. Each describes its problem in words written by hand before any matcher: one wording to tune, another to report. These numbers use the report wording.
Accuracy
The right knowledge came back, or nothing when nothing applies, and no noise. Solution found counts only cases where knowledge applies.
Problem relevance
Of the problem's words that the knowledge base uses, the share of their weight a rule mentions, after a small field thesaurus (SFP = transceiver, reboot = restart). Threshold —, chosen on the tuning wording as the most accurate one that keeps at least 97% of solutions. Nothing is dropped: rules about another problem stay listed after the matching ones.
Naive RAG
BM25 over whole threads with channel and subject, the same thesaurus, element ids as tokens, top 5, over all knowledge threads plus a 10% sample of the rest (— threads). A bigger haystack can only make it worse.
Left out
The LLM extractor at scale (no calls were made); dense embeddings (a word-based matcher was used, so paraphrases with no shared concept are missed); real Elisa data. The language of the dataset is template-based, so the absolute numbers are optimistic, while the gaps between pipelines are the finding.
